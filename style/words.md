# The style guide: word choice

This pass changes words and short phrases. It does not touch sentence shape or
document shape; other passes do that.

## The principles that bear on word choice

1. Write words people like.
2. Verbs are better than nouns. Nouns are better than adjectives and adverbs.
3. Avoid new words as they will likely be short-lived.
4. Avoid complex nouns formed by adding prefixes and suffixes to verbs.
5. Choose substance over style, even if it contradicts any of these rules.

## Attack nominalization

Words like "initialization" should not be part of text you edit. They should be rewritten using the verb they nominalized, like "start".

## No hedging

It doesn't matter what the topic is, avoid hedging language like "may", "might", "could", "seems", "appears", etc. Be direct and assertive and list specific counterexamples only when strictly necessary. Text is about making a point, excessive hedging detracts from that.

Hedging = less persuasive writing, and most of the time writing is trying to convince people of something.

## Gendered pronouns for a generic person

"He/she" and "him/her" are unsayable aloud.

If the person is known, name them. "The person indicates that he/she consents" → "The man consents".
If the person is generic, use the plural. "When each student receives the exam, he/she may not leave" → "After receiving their exams, students may not leave".
