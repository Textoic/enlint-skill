# The style guide: sentence structure

This pass rebuilds sentences. The words have already been chosen and the
paragraphs have already been arranged; what is left is how each sentence is
built.

## The principles that bear on sentence structure

1. Be direct with the information you want to communicate. Don't be roundabout.
2. Use the active voice.
3. One idea per sentence.
4. Affirmative is better than negative.
5. Avoid similes and metaphors. When explaining a complex idea just break it down into simple ideas.
6. Choose substance over style, even if it contradicts any of these rules.

## Avoid contrasts saying both what something is and isn't

Three main patterns you need to rewrite when you see them:

1. **X is not just A, but also B**: "He is not just a teacher, but also a coach" → "He is a teacher and a coach".
2. **X is not A, but B**: "The thing that blows up is not the deficit; it's the debt" → "But it's the debt that blows up".
3. **X is A, not B**: "It reads as an encyclopedia entry, not a story" → "It reads as an encyclopedia entry".

## Don't use bursts of three everywhere

Rewrite patterns where 3 actions, benefits or characteristics are presented in short sequence to keep only the key one and discard the rest. Examples:

- "fast, reliable, and scalable."
- "plan, execute, and optimize."

## Don't append adjectives as a delimited phrase after the noun

Phrases describing a noun with adjectives after the noun, usually introduced with delimiters like dashes, commas, or parentheses, need to be removed.

Example: "Frontal passage doesn't just rotate the wind, it usually punches it up — gusty, stronger, and often drier behind the front" should become "Frontal passage rotates the wind and often makes it gusty."

## Avoid putting adjectives in parenthesis next to the noun

This is an anti-pattern to use as a header or list time:
**Patent (Expensive, Uncertain, Powerful)**

Instead say why it's expensive, uncertain, and powerful in prose and maybe **bold** the key points.

## Don't end sentences with a trailing gerund phrase

Rewrite any sentence where where the consequence is presented as a gerund at the end, oten introduced by a comma. Examples of the pattern and how it could be rewritten:

- "Automation massively cuts down costs**, enabling teams to scale**" => "Automation helps teams scale by cutting costs massively"
- "The measure brought little improvement in living conditions**, highlighting the need for a more nuanced approach**" => "The measure failed to improve living conditions and we need something better"

## Vary sentence length and structure

Constantly using long sentences, and repeating the same patterns throughout a text makes it boring, monotonous and quickly loses readers' attention.

Change up sentence length and construction frequently, throw in **bold** formatting and _italic_ on key ideas in the middle of paragraphs to draw attention to them.

Follow up a long sentence with a short one.
